# Atomic Finds ATX — Sales Model & Platform Plan

**Version:** Phase 1 recommendation

**Working premise:** Atomic Finds ATX currently sells through Jennyfer’s personal Facebook Marketplace profile. The website is a pilot and should become the branded showroom, selected-inventory preview, trust builder, and inquiry hub—not a full ecommerce store—until inventory records, policies, and operations are stable.

---

## 1. Recommended Sales Model

### Phase 1: Showroom + Marketplace Bridge

**Customer promise**

Atomic Finds ATX curates vintage furniture and décor with warmth, character, and a story. Visitors can discover current finds, see how pieces live in real spaces, and follow the link or inquiry path that leads to the live sales channel.

**Channel responsibilities**

| Channel | Primary job | Customer action | Owner/update cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website | Brand home, selected inventory, product context, trust, inquiry | Explore, learn, ask about a find, follow to active listing | Update available features at least weekly and immediately after a featured item sells |
| Facebook Marketplace | Real-time listings and sales conversion | View full listing, message, confirm purchase/pickup | Update at listing, pending, sold, and removal stages |
| Instagram | Discovery, storytelling, repeat attention, social proof | Follow, DM, view drops, visit website/Marketplace | 2–3 Reels/posts per week plus Stories as inventory/work happens |
| Email newsletter | Future drop alerts and relationship building | Subscribe for new finds | Do not launch until the form and consent flow work |
| Facebook Page | Future brand identity/community layer | Follow, message, view credibility content | Establish later if Jenny wants a separate business-facing presence |
| Google Business Profile | Future local discovery/credibility | Search, call/message, view business details | Create only after public business details and customer process are settled |

### Customer flow

1. A person discovers Atomic Finds ATX through Instagram, Marketplace, a shared link, or search.
2. They land on the website to understand the brand and view selected pieces.
3. They see a current item with its name, photos, condition, dimensions, status, and clear next step.
4. They either open the specific Marketplace listing or use a product-specific inquiry form.
5. Jennyfer confirms availability and follows her approved purchase/pickup policy.
6. When a find sells, it becomes **Adopted** and can move into a portfolio/archive instead of remaining available.

### Non-negotiable operating rule

> Never show an item as Available on the website unless someone has ownership of updating its status after a Marketplace sale.

If updating a live inventory selection becomes too difficult, the website should feature a “Recent Finds” gallery and direct visitors to Marketplace/Instagram for current availability rather than operating as a catalog.

## 3. Inventory and SKU Integration Plan

Your upcoming SKU system should be the source of truth for product content across every channel.

### One-record rule

Each physical piece gets one internal inventory record. The website, Marketplace listing, Instagram post, price, condition details, photo folder, and sale status all point back to that record.

### Recommended field mapping

| Inventory field | Website | Marketplace | Instagram |
|---|---|---|---|
| SKU | Optional internal reference / form prefill | Internal notes or end of listing if useful | Internal planning only |
| Public name | Product title | In title/body | Caption/reel title |
| Status | Available/Pending/Adopted | Listing state | Available/Sold Story/Highlight |
| Price | Show only if current | Required | Optional |
| Dimensions | Product details | Required | Optional carousel slide/caption |
| Materials | Product details | Required | Caption/details |
| Condition/wear | Product details | Required | Caption only if relevant |
| Work completed | Product details | Listing details | Process content |
| Marketplace URL | CTA | N/A | Link-in-bio / DM reference |
| Website URL | N/A | Optional in seller message | Link-in-bio/Stories |
| Photos | Gallery | Listing images | Feed/Reel/Story assets |
| Sale date | Archive | Sold/remove status | Sold celebration |

### Status workflow

1. **Acquired** — internal only; not yet photographed/prepared.
2. **In Prep** — being cleaned, assessed, repaired, or staged.
3. **Ready to List** — record and photo set complete.
4. **Available** — active Marketplace listing; may appear on website.
5. **Pending** — buyer/deposit/hold state; website should stop inviting immediate purchase.
6. **Adopted** — sold; remove purchase CTA and optionally keep in archive.
7. **Archived** — retained as portfolio/story only.

## 4. 30-Day Practical Plan

### Week 1: Make public claims safe

- Remove delivery and white-glove claims.
- Identify which product cards are placeholders versus real/available.
- Confirm or hide all ratings, testimonials, social metrics, hours, contact details, and policy claims.
- Agree on the Phase 1 customer flow: website → specific Marketplace listing or product inquiry → pickup.

### Week 2: Build the product source of truth

- Finalize the SKU format and inventory sheet/database.
- Add existing featured products as test records.
- Create folder/naming standards for photos.
- Capture product-specific Marketplace URLs.
- Assign each item a status.

### Week 3: Rebuild content around verified records

- Update featured product cards using verified inventory data.
- Add How to Buy and revised FAQs.
- Replace general Marketplace links.
- Move sold/placeholder pieces into an archive or remove them.
- Create one reusable Marketplace description template and one Instagram caption template.

### Week 4: Build trust and repeatability

- Verify approved testimonials.
- Add real, permitted styled-home content.
- Create a small Instagram content batch from real inventory.
- Decide whether the email form is ready to connect.
- Create a launch checklist and assign ongoing update ownership.

## 6. Phase 1 Success Criteria

The system is ready for a practical V1 launch when:

- [ ] The website never promises delivery or services that do not exist.
- [ ] At least 3–6 featured pieces have verified records and accurate statuses.
- [ ] Every active website item has a specific Marketplace link or product-specific inquiry flow.
- [ ] The buyer can understand how to purchase and pick up an item in under one minute.
- [ ] Product details match the Marketplace listing and inventory source of truth.
- [ ] Sold items are updated within a defined timeframe.
- [ ] All testimonials and public proof are verifiable.
- [ ] Newsletter and social components are either real/functioning or intentionally deferred.
- [ ] Someone is assigned to update availability after a sale.
